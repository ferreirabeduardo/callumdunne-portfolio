Belong ToStand Up Awareness Week

Over three quarters of Irish secondary schools stood against LGBTQ+ bullying. I helped make sure they heard why it mattered.

Every year, Belong To runs Stand Up Awareness Week to get Irish secondary schools talking openly about LGBTQ+ bullying, and every year, IAPI's member agencies volunteer their time to help spread it. In 2022, the final year of that partnership, I was put forward by TBWA\Ireland to lead community management for the campaign, working alongside people from fourteen different agencies, producers, videographers, stylists, and a lot of people behind the scenes, all giving up their time for free.

That year's message was “words that heal,” built from a simple, difficult question: what did LGBTQ+ people wish they'd actually heard in school, instead of what they did hear. I ran the community side of the campaign as it went live, across every platform, making sure the message reached as many young people, students, teachers and schools as it possibly could.

This project was personal for me. I gave up my time and skills for this because I've seen firsthand why this kind of visibility matters, especially somewhere as public and permanent as a secondary school corridor.

It reached further than any year before it. Participation grew from under half of Ireland's second-level schools in 2018 to over three quarters by 2022, and the number of students reached nearly doubled in that time, from 162,000 to 312,971. Seeing the campaign go live across social media was one thing. Finding out it had been postered up in my own hometown secondary school was another entirely, a small, specific proof that LGBTQIA+ youths in Ireland today have support that simply didn't exist when I was in school myself.

Some campaigns are measured in impressions. This one was measured in whether a student walking down a corridor felt a little less alone, understood and supported.
