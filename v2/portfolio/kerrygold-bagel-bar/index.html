Kerrygold IrelandBagel Bar

Kerrygold's first activation in its home market. Every launch after it followed the same playbook.

Kerrygold had only ever sold one thing in Ireland: butter. Sixty years of it, the brand people reached for without thinking twice. So when the business decided to launch a cream cheese, this was actually Kerrygold's first product launch outside butter in its own home market, which is a bigger deal than it sounds.

I didn't think a press release and a shelf display were going to convince anyone of anything. You don't tell the people a new product is good. They've heard that pitch from every brand that's ever launched something. You have to hand people a reason to believe it themselves.

At the same time, there was a small but loud conversation happening online at the time. Dublin creators and locals kept saying the same thing: you couldn't get a proper bagel anywhere in the city, while New York-style cream cheese bagels kept doing the rounds on social feeds. Nobody local had filled that gap. So I took over a storefront on Camden Street for three days, brought in Dublin's most loved bagels from Bretzel Bakery, and gave away 1,200 of them, loaded with Kerrygold's new cream cheese, 151kg of it across the three days, completely free, of course.

People queued from the first morning, hours in advance. By the third day, we'd had hundreds of messages asking if we'd ever open somewhere permanent. That's the reaction I actually trust: not a launch plan working out on paper, but people asking for more of something that didn't exist the week before.

This was the first time Kerrygold had ever done a pop-up or activation in its home market—and since then, every product launch that came after it followed the exact same activation approach.

The numbers held up too. 3.4M organic reach, 513K earned media impressions, international press pickup, and a 4.2% week-on-week retail uplift in Dublin within days of the pop-up closing. A three-day gap in the Irish food scene turned into the reason people tried a product that hadn't existed there a week earlier.

Give people something worth queuing for, and they'll do the convincing for you.
